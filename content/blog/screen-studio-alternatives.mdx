**Short answer:** on **Windows**, try **FocuSee** (auto zoom, Windows and Mac) or
**Cap** (open source, Windows, Mac and Linux). For **free**, start with **Cap's
free plan**, the **Screenity** Chrome extension or **OBS Studio**. If you want a
camera and an editor, **Tella**. If the video should live in code and be
re-rendered, **Remotion**.

Everything below was checked on each product's own site in October 2026.

## Why look for an alternative

[Screen Studio](https://screen.studio) describes itself as "the screen recorder
for Mac that handles the hard parts" — zooms, motion, sound and framing. It is
**macOS only**: its FAQ says "Currently, Screen Studio only supports macOS" and
that there are no near-future plans to add Windows support. It costs $29 a month,
or $9 a month billed yearly. The usual reasons to look elsewhere are a Windows
machine, wanting something free, or wanting a camera track and an editor.

## FocuSee

FocuSee calls itself an "AI-powered screen recorder with auto zoom effects", the
closest match to Screen Studio's pitch. It supports **Windows and macOS**. Its
store lists Standard at $19.99 a month or $49.99 a year for one computer, and
Advanced at $24.99 a month, $79.99 a year, or $199.99 once for a lifetime licence
covering up to five computers. The free trial lets you export one video; some AI
features need credits.

**Pick it if:** you are on Windows and want the Screen Studio style of automatic
zooms.

## Cap

Cap is an open-source recorder; most of its code is AGPLv3 according to its
GitHub repository. The download page offers macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel),
Windows and Linux builds. The **free plan** covers personal use, including Studio
Mode and 4K/60fps export, but not commercial use. Studio Mode's editor
advertises automatic zoom from your recorded clicks, cursor smoothing and click
effects, and custom backgrounds. The Desktop License is $29 a
year or $58 once, and Cap Pro — cloud sharing, AI titles and transcripts — is $12
per user per month, or $8.16 billed annually.

**Pick it if:** you want a free or one-time-price recorder, or the source code.

## Tella

Tella records screen, camera, microphone and system audio as separate tracks, and
can automatically add layouts, backgrounds, zoom effects and subtitles. It runs on
**Mac, Windows**, in the browser and as a Chrome extension, and exports up to 4K
at 60fps. Pro is $13 and Premium $19 per user per month, billed annually; there is
no free plan, though the FAQ mentions a trial.

**Pick it if:** you want yourself on camera next to the product and an editor to
cut it.

## Screenity

Screenity is a screen recorder and video editor. The recorder is a **Chrome
extension** that is free and open source under GPLv3, with a cursor spotlight. The
paid editor ($10 a month, or $120 a year, with a 7-day trial) turns your clicks
into zooms you can retime or remove.

**Pick it if:** you only need to record browser tabs and want it free.

## OBS Studio

OBS Studio is "free and open source software for video recording and live
streaming", on **Windows, macOS and Linux**. It is a general recorder, not a demo
polisher: you set up the scenes and do the zooms in an editor afterwards.

**Pick it if:** you need free, full control and do not mind editing separately.

## Loom

Loom records screen and camera for async video messages, on Mac, Windows, iOS and
Android. The free Starter plan allows 25 videos of up to five minutes at up to
720p; Business is $18 per user per month. It is built for sharing a quick
explanation, not for a polished launch video.

**Pick it if:** the video is a message to a teammate or customer.

## Or build the video in code

A recorder gives you a finished clip. If you would rather have the video in your
repo — editable text, re-rendered each release —
[Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev) renders React components into a video. Its
license is free for individuals, for-profit companies with up to three employees
and non-profits; larger companies need a company license.

You can still start from a recording. snapcn (our library, so read with that in
mind) has a [screen-recording scene](/docs/screens/screen-recording) that frames
a clip with a camera move and a [cursor track](/docs/screens/cursor-track), and
both install with `npx shadcn@latest add @snapcn/<name>`, copying the source into
your project. About 48 components are free under MIT. Recording in Cap or OBS and
finishing in Remotion is a reasonable free pipeline. The full walkthrough is
[how to make a product demo video in React](/product-demo-video-react), and for a
looping README clip see [the GitHub demo GIF](/github-readme-demo-gif).

## How to choose

- **Windows, want auto zoom:** FocuSee or Cap.
- **Free:** Cap free plan (personal use), Screenity extension, OBS Studio.
- **Camera plus editing:** Tella.
- **Quick message:** Loom.
- **Re-rendered every release, motion graphics around the recording:** Remotion.
