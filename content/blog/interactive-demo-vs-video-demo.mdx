**Short answer:** make a **video** when the viewer is passive — scrolling a
feed, landing on your homepage, watching a launch. Make an **interactive demo**
when the viewer is actively evaluating — a buyer who wants to try before a call,
or a new user learning a workflow. Most products end up with both: a 30–60
second video at the top, and a clickable demo for the people who want more.

Tool details below were checked on each vendor's site in October 2026. The
advice sections are our own judgement, not survey data.

## At a glance

| | Video demo | Interactive demo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer | Watches | Clicks through |
| Pace | Yours | Theirs |
| Works in a feed (X, LinkedIn) | Yes, as a video post | Needs a link out |
| Product Hunt gallery | Yes, as a YouTube link | Yes, as a demo link |
| Landing page | Top of the page | Lower down, or behind "try it" |
| Sales | Before the first call | During and after evaluation |
| Onboarding | Overview | Step-by-step guides |
| Tools | Screen Studio, Tella, Loom, Cap, Remotion | Arcade, Supademo, Storylane |

## What each one is

A **video demo** is a file — usually an MP4 — that plays the same way for everyone.
You choose what is shown, in what order and how fast. It can be a cleaned-up screen
recording, a camera walkthrough, or a motion-graphics cut built from scratch.

An **interactive demo** is a captured copy of your product that the viewer steps
through. Arcade describes recording with a Chrome extension, desktop app or Figma
plugin and then adding hotspots, callouts, voiceover and branching. Storylane also
offers "sandbox demos" that "let buyers and sellers navigate a pixel-perfect clone
of the product." The viewer is driving, so the demo has to survive them clicking
in an order you did not plan.

## When a video wins

**Launch posts on X and LinkedIn.** A feed post carries a video that plays in
place. A clickable demo has to be a link to somewhere else, and a link is a
second decision the scroller has to make. For the launch moment, the video is the
asset; see [how to make a Product Hunt launch video](/product-hunt-launch-video)
for the structure and the aspect ratios.

**Product Hunt.** Product Hunt's
[posting guide](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product)
says gallery videos must be YouTube links, and that it needs at least two gallery
images. It also lists interactive demo tools — Arcade, Storylane, ScreenSpace,
Hexus, Supademo and Layerpath — whose demos can be added to the gallery as a link.
So you can ship both; the video is what plays first.

**The top of a landing page.** A first-time visitor has not decided to invest any
effort yet. A short video asks nothing of them — it can autoplay muted, it shows the
outcome, and it is over in under a minute. An interactive demo asks them to start
clicking before they know whether they care.

**A README or docs page.** A looping GIF shows the product right where the
reader already is, with no click. See
[the GitHub README demo GIF](/github-readme-demo-gif).

**When the product is hard to show by clicking.** Anything that happens over
time — an AI answer streaming in, a build running in a terminal, a background job
finishing — is easier to stage as a video than as a sequence of click targets.

## When an interactive demo wins

**A buyer evaluating before a sales call.** Storylane's pitch is exactly this:
"Interactive demos open your product before a form or a call." Supademo lists
landing-page embeds so visitors "try the product before booking" a call, and
personalised demos in outbound sequences. A buyer comparing three tools wants to
poke at the real flow, not watch your edit of it.

**Onboarding a specific workflow.** Supademo describes helping new customers
"complete key workflows with interactive demos they can follow at their own pace."
A step-by-step guide beats a video here because the user can stop on the step they
are stuck on.

**When sales wants to know who looked.** Interactive demo platforms build analytics
in — Supademo lists tracking links and analytics on its paid plan, and Storylane's
Starter plan includes HubSpot and Zapier integrations. If the demo is a
sales-pipeline tool, that tracking is the point.

## The tradeoffs

| | Video | Interactive demo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Control of the story | Full | Shared with the viewer |
| Effort for the viewer | None | They have to click |
| Keeping it current | Re-record or re-render | Re-capture the changed steps |
| Motion and titles | Yes | Mostly callouts and hotspots |
| Where it plays | Anywhere a video plays | Where the embed or link works |

Two practical points. First, a video goes stale when the UI changes; an
interactive demo does too, step by step. If you ship UI every week, pick the
format whose update path you will actually do. Second, many interactive tools
also export video — Arcade says any demo can be exported "as an MP4 video, a GIF,
or a step-by-step guide", and Supademo's FAQ says it "exports in GIF, MP4, and PDF
formats" — so capturing once can cover both, at the cost of a video that looks
like a click-through.

## Tools for each

**Video:**

- [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio) — macOS-only recorder with automatic
  zooms; $29 a month or $9 a month billed yearly.
- [Tella](https://www.tella.com) — screen and camera on separate tracks, with an
  editor; Mac, Windows and browser.
- [Loom](https://www.loom.com) — quick async video messages; free Starter plan.
- [Cap](https://cap.so) — open-source recorder with a free personal-use plan.
- [Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev) — videos written as React components,
  rendered to a file. Free for individuals and companies up to three employees.

If you build the video in code, prebuilt scenes save you the motion design.
snapcn is our library of them, so read this with that in mind: shadcn-installed
Remotion components for software videos such as a
[prompt being typed and sent](/docs/ai-input/prompt-send), an
[answer streaming in](/docs/ai-input/answer-stream), a
[terminal](/docs/screens/terminal-simulator) and a
[framed screen recording](/docs/screens/screen-recording). About 48 are free under
MIT, and there is a [browser video editor](/docs/video-editor) if you would rather
not set up a project. The step-by-step build is in
[how to make a product demo video in React](/product-demo-video-react).

**Interactive:**

- [Arcade](https://www.arcade.software) — Chrome extension, desktop app or Figma
  plugin; exports MP4 and GIF; free plan, Pro from $49 a month billed annually.
- [Supademo](https://supademo.com) — Chrome extension and desktop apps; free plan
  with five interactive demos; Scale from $38 a month billed annually.
- [Storylane](https://www.storylane.io) — interactive and sandbox demos; free plan
  with one live demo; Starter $50 a month.

The full comparison, with pricing for each, is in

## A sensible default

1. Make one 30–60 second video of the core job. Put it at the top of the landing
   page, in the launch post and in the Product Hunt gallery.
2. If buyers evaluate before talking to you, or onboarding has a workflow people
   get stuck in, add an interactive demo for that one flow.
3. Update whichever one goes stale first; that is the one people are using.
