**Short answer:** for a polished screen recording on a Mac, **Screen Studio**; on
Mac or Windows, **Tella** or **Cap**; for a quick async walkthrough, **Loom**. If
you want people to click through the product rather than watch it, use an
interactive demo tool — **Arcade**, **Supademo** or **Storylane**. If you want the
video to live in your repo and re-render when the product changes, build it in
code with **Remotion**.

Prices and platforms below were checked on each tool's own site in October 2026.
They change often, so confirm on the pricing page before you buy.

## Screen recorders

### Screen Studio

Screen Studio calls itself "the screen recorder for Mac that handles the hard
parts" — zooms, motion, sound and framing — so a raw recording comes out looking
edited. It is macOS only; its FAQ says there are no near-future plans for
Windows. Pricing is $29 a month, or $9 a month billed yearly, and every feature
is in every plan.

**Best for:** a Mac user who wants a polished recording without opening an
editor. If you are on Windows, see our
[Screen Studio alternatives](/screen-studio-alternatives).

### Tella

Tella records your screen, camera, microphone and system audio as separate
tracks, so you can change the layout after recording, and its editor handles
trimming, layouts, zooms, captions and sound cleanup. It runs on Mac and Windows,
in the browser, and as a Chrome extension. The pricing page lists Pro at $13 per
user per month and Premium at $19, both billed annually; there is no free plan,
though the FAQ mentions a free trial.

**Best for:** a walkthrough with you on camera that needs light editing.

### Loom

Loom is for recording and sharing video messages with teammates and customers.
It records your screen and camera, on Mac, Windows, iOS and Android. The free
Starter plan allows 25 videos of up to five minutes each, at up to 720p. Business
is $18 per user per month with unlimited videos and recording time, and Business
+ AI is $24.

**Best for:** a quick explanation sent to one person or a team, not a launch
video.

### Cap

Cap is an open-source screen recorder. Most of the code is AGPLv3, according to
its GitHub repository. The download page offers macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel),
Windows and Linux builds. The free plan is for personal use; commercial use needs
a paid plan. The Desktop License is $29 a year or $58 once for lifetime, and Cap
Pro, which adds cloud sharing and AI titles and transcripts, is $12 per user per
month or $8.16 billed annually.

**Best for:** a low-cost or open-source recorder, including on Windows and Linux.

## Interactive demo tools

These capture your product as clickable steps rather than a video. We cover when
that is the right format in
[interactive demo vs video demo](/interactive-demo-vs-video-demo).

### Arcade

Arcade records with a Chrome extension, a desktop app or a Figma plugin, then lets
you add hotspots, callouts, voiceover and branching. Demos embed on websites, help
centres and inside your product, and any demo can be exported as an MP4, a GIF or
a step-by-step guide. Arcade's homepage now leads with an AI video generator for
marketing teams, and its pricing page lists credit-based plans: a free plan and
Pro from $49 a month billed annually.

**Best for:** a clickable demo that you also want as a video or GIF.

### Supademo

Supademo captures demos with a Chrome extension or Mac and Windows desktop apps.
Its site lists website, sales and onboarding use cases, and its FAQ says demos
export as GIF, MP4 and PDF. The free plan includes five interactive demos for one
creator. Scale is $50 a month, or $38 a month billed annually, per creator.

**Best for:** a small team that wants interactive demos on a free or low tier.

### Storylane

Storylane offers interactive demos, sandbox demos ("a pixel-perfect clone of the
product") and Hubs, a digital sales room. The free plan allows one published demo
at a time. Starter is listed at $50 a month, with a 20% discount for yearly
billing; Growth and Premium are priced for sales teams.

**Best for:** a sales-led company that wants demos inside a sales process.

## Code-based: Remotion and component libraries

[Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev) describes itself as a way to "create videos
and motion graphics with React". Each scene is a React component, you preview it in
Remotion Studio, and `npx remotion render` writes the file (H.264 is the default
codec). Remotion's license is free for individuals, for-profit companies with up
to three employees, and non-profits; larger companies need a company license.

What you get is a demo you can edit like code: change a string, re-render, and the
video matches the new UI. What you pay is setup time and the motion design itself,
which is where component libraries come in. We compare them in
[the best Remotion component libraries](/best-remotion-component-libraries).

[snapcn](/docs/components) is ours, so weigh this paragraph accordingly. It is a
shadcn registry of Remotion components built for software videos — a
[screen recording with a camera move](/docs/screens/screen-recording), a
[cursor track](/docs/screens/cursor-track), a
[prompt being typed](/docs/ai-input/prompt-send), a
[laptop frame](/docs/screens/laptop-frame). You install one with
`npx shadcn@latest add @snapcn/<name>` and the source is copied into your project.
About 48 components are free under MIT; Pro is paid. There is also a
[browser video editor](/docs/video-editor) if you want to assemble scenes without
a Remotion project.

**Best for:** developers who want the demo video in their repo, re-rendered as the
product changes. Walkthroughs:
[product demo video in React](/product-demo-video-react) and
[a SaaS demo video without After Effects](/saas-demo-video-without-after-effects).

## How to choose

1. **Do viewers need to click through the product?** Use an interactive demo tool.
2. **Are you recording real UI on a Mac and want it polished?** Screen Studio.
3. **On Windows or Linux, or want it free?** Cap; Tella if you want an editor
   with a camera.
4. **Is it a quick note to a teammate or customer?** Loom.
5. **Will the video be re-made every release, or does it need motion graphics?**
   Remotion, with prebuilt components.

The categories mix well: record the product in Screen Studio or Cap, then bring
the clip into a Remotion composition for the titles and the cut. For launch
specifics see [the Product Hunt launch video](/product-hunt-launch-video), and
for a README see [the GitHub demo GIF](/github-readme-demo-gif).
